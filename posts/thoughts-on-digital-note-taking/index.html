Thoughts on Digital Note Taking

educationnotetakingstrategynotionmindset

Since high school, I’ve been taking notes when I sense I need them. This sense was like a thought on my mind like “I’d better be writing this” or “Instructor is now speaking about something important and I cannot miss that”. But, when I started studying computer engineering at university it changed a little bit to “That seems like information worth writing in my notebook.” It’s a bit narrowed perspective compared to the others because what is technically worth and not is up to me and in university, you are taking very specified courses that is hard to decide the result of this “worthy” decision.

For a couple of years from high school to university, I couldn’t put in order this note-taking strategy to be both efficient and information loss-free. During this time, I would like to share a note I took:

“Software development life cycle: Requirements, design, implementation, testing, maintenance.”

It contains (or paraphrases) some information and when I need to study for an exam or prepare myself for this course, I will come to that note and just read that. Then I will begin thinking that I know that information because I took note of it in the course. But there was an important thing about my notes, they are the lines I decided to worth writing, I cannot remember why I wrote them or if there was any more information that I found not worth but it was worth it at that time. It’s important to keep in mind that people can’t write as quickly as they can talk.

Modifications

So this situation is a vulnerability for me to trust and extract results from my notes. Because they can contain mistakes, are less readable, and can include wrong or missing information. After that, I decided to use my notes as writing references to data instead of becoming information itself. Each line of note should redirect the reader to the target data. Instead of giving the whole information about the topic, I began to write little references such as “Software Dev. Life Cycle parts” or “SQL Operators”. The trick is that no matter if I cannot write the data correctly or not in the course, I can google my notes and find the information related to them without thinking “What if I missed something?”

With that strategy, you can both take more notes by deciding worth it or not and referencing the suitable topics you can come to their target information whenever you want. (It’s like the Pointers concept for software engineering who reads this).

This is just the beginning of note-taking. For a few months, I switched to digital note-taking.

Digital Version of Note-Taking Strategy

The main difference between digital and classic (or analog) note-taking, and also the biggest advantage of digital is the keyboard: No one can type faster than a keyboard by typing by hand. By considering the arguments above, this is a huge advantage over classic note-taking.

On the other hand, it has some disadvantages caused by over-developed note apps.

Simplicity

While the digital age has introduced us to a plethora of note-taking applications, it’s crucial to remember that the essence of effective note-taking lies not in the app’s bells and whistles, but in its ability to seamlessly integrate into your daily life. Rather than getting caught up in the endless quest to find the perfect app, consider adopting a note-taking approach rooted in simplicity and focused on fulfilling your core needs.

For all note-taking apps on the market, there are 3 top-tier functions needed to satisfy 80% of your noting expectations:

  • Todo item
  • List item (ordered/non-ordered)
  • Synchronization across devices

Other than that abilities are too much basic needs and they are built for complex usage cases. If you have simple needs for complex note-taking processes, this trio is what you need.

Consider Notion, a great tool to organize whatever you want on texts, tables, complex data sets, etc. but if you want to just write some todo’s about your day, keep a book list to read, or notes about your recent job applications, the Notion can be overqualified for this tasks if you try to use all of its abilities, it is nonsense.

Beyond these three essential functions, many note-taking apps offer a plethora of additional features designed for more complex use cases. However, for those with straightforward note-taking needs, such as recording tasks, creating simple lists, and ensuring that notes are accessible on various devices, these extras can often become distractions rather than enhancements. In such scenarios, embracing the concept of simplicity in your digital note-taking routine can prove to be far more efficient and satisfying.

Conclusion

Ultimately, the key to successful digital note-taking is not obsessing over the latest and greatest app, but rather finding a tool that complements your workflow and keeps your note-taking process straightforward and efficient. By focusing on the essentials and avoiding unnecessary complexity, you can optimize your note-taking experience and maintain clarity and organization in your daily life. So, remember, it’s not about the app; it’s about how you use it to enhance your productivity and achieve your goals.
