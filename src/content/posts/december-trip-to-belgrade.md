After nearly a decade without traveling abroad, I found that a brief trip can truly replenish your energy and broaden your perspective. This marks my first travel blog entry, and I’m thrilled to share my experiences in one of the Balkans’ understated treasures: Belgrade, Serbia 🇷🇸.

### Why Belgrade?

When selecting a destination, my primary consideration was the ease of travel. As a Turkish citizen, visa-free options are somewhat limited. Of course, there is the “Special Passport” option — granted to state employees and their families — which opens the door to over a hundred countries without the hassle of extra paperwork. For those of us without one, Europe becomes a narrower field. After some research, I decided that Belgrade was an accessible, intriguing choice. Before heading out, my friend and I booked a cozy Airbnb apartment and nabbed some budget-friendly airline tickets, excited about what lay ahead.

### Setting the Stage

We planned a relaxed itinerary: exploring the city’s main attractions on foot, tracing walking routes through the heart of Old Belgrade, and soaking in its authentic atmosphere. The idea was to see as much as we could by just strolling around, without feeling rushed. This being my very first international adventure, I was a mix of nerves and excitement. Another pleasant surprise was the overall affordability. For four full days, including comfortable accommodation, inexpensive flights, meals, souvenirs, and even a few indulgent dinners, I spent roughly €360 (~13,000 TRY). Not bad for a European getaway, especially when you factor in the charming streets, hearty food, and warm culture.

### First Impressions and Getting Around

Touching down at Nikola Tesla Airport, we were immediately greeted by the shouts of “Taxi!!” echoing through the arrival hall. This can be overwhelming if you’re not expecting it. Instead of giving in, we stuck to the plan and looked for the A1 Shuttle bus. It’s a distinctive, red-colored city bus and a reliable, low-cost way to reach the center. The fare is only 400 RSD (approximately 125 TRY or €3.4), but remember, they only take cash — no cards allowed. Speaking of currency, Serbia uses the Serbian Dinar (RSD), and the exchange rate hovers around 116 RSD per €1. Before you know it, you’ll start getting used to handling the colorful notes and doing quick mental conversions.

### Cash or Card

Although carrying some cash (Serbian Dinar) is always useful in Belgrade — especially for smaller souvenir shops or local bakeries — I found that most restaurants, cafes, and larger stores readily accept both credit cards and contactless payment. That said, it’s still a good idea to keep some cash on hand for those charming, hole-in-the-wall places that only take bills and coins. Souvenir stalls in particular often lack card terminals, so if you’re planning to pick up trinkets or gifts, be prepared to pay cash.

### The City and Arrival

Once you arrive at the airport, the A2 Shuttle will take you to Slavija Square (“Trg” in Serbian). This bustling hub is home to several hotels (including the Hilton), plenty of pizza joints, and numerous shops — making it a convenient base if you plan to stay nearby. I recommend heading straight to your accommodation to drop off your bags so you can start exploring right away.

December days in Belgrade are short, with sunset around **3:55 PM**, and the city tends to quiet down after about **7:00 PM**. To make the most of the daylight, aim to get up at **8:00 AM**, venture outside, and breathe in the crisp morning air before the streets get busy. One of the best ways to begin your day is by stopping at a local **Pekara** (bakery) to pick up some fresh pastries or traditional burek. It’s a quick, tasty, and wallet-friendly breakfast that will fuel you for a full day of sightseeing.

### Places To Visit

Belgrade offers a mix of historical and modern attractions, many of which are walkable from Slavija Square or easily accessible via public transport. Most walking routes will lead you through the city’s heart, allowing you to immerse yourself in the local atmosphere — pop into cafes for a coffee break, browse through souvenir stalls, and simply soak up the lively vibe. It’s a city that reveals its charm step by step, and you’ll soon discover why Belgrade is considered a hidden gem in the Balkans.

### Kalemegdan

One of the must-see spots in Belgrade is the magnificent Kalemegdan Fortress, situated where the Sava and Danube Rivers meet. It’s the city’s largest park and historical complex, offering panoramic views over the riverbanks and the skyline. As you stroll along the ancient fortress walls, take a moment to soak up the history — from the centuries-old gates to the expansive green areas that locals use as a relaxing retreat. Kalemegdan is also perfect for a leisurely walk or a casual photo session at sunset (remember, daylight fades early in December). It’s an ideal first stop to get a feel for Belgrade’s layered past and vibrant present all in one place.

### Skadarlija

If you want to experience Belgrade’s bohemian spirit, make your way to Skadarlija — a cobblestone street often likened to Paris’s Montmartre. Lined with traditional restaurants, lively cafés, and charming bars, Skadarlija feels like stepping into another era. In the evening, it’s common to hear local folk musicians performing familiar tunes, adding an extra layer of warmth to the bustling atmosphere. Whether you’re in search of hearty Serbian dishes or simply want to enjoy a glass of rakija while people-watching, Skadarlija offers a blend of nostalgia and fun that epitomizes Belgrade’s vibrant, artistic soul.

### St. Marks Place

After a day of exploring, consider indulging in a meal at **St. Mark’s Place**, a cozy restaurant known for its traditional Serbian dishes and warm ambiance. Situated close to St. Mark’s Church, this spot offers everything from hearty local specialties (The lasagna was also perfect.) to a variety of regional wines. The friendly staff is more than happy to help you navigate the menu and recommend the perfect dish or rakija pairing. Whether you’re a foodie eager to dive into Serbian flavors or simply seeking a relaxing dinner after sightseeing, St. Mark’s Place is a must-try dining experience in Belgrade.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*x_xXtjMXGKVcS67wie0rPw.jpeg)

Whether you’re wandering through the historic halls of Kalemegdan Fortress, soaking in the bohemian charm of Skadarlija, or savoring traditional Serbian fare at St. Mark’s Place, Belgrade offers an enriching blend of history, culture, and modern vibrancy. Despite the short December days and the early quiet in the evenings, the city brims with warmth and character at every turn. For a budget-friendly European getaway with an authentic local feel, Belgrade truly stands out as a hidden gem in the Balkans — one that will leave you refreshed, inspired, and already planning a return visit.
