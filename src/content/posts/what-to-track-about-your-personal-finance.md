A typical person can whether get richer, or get poorer by time. It’s pretty simple as that. A person is either economically positive or negative at the end of the day. Both positive/negative days gets together and creates whether a positive or negative month. Then months make up years and years make up your life.

An average person can live up to ~26.000 days, which makes ~71 years nearly in his/her whole life. If we’re talking about “Personal” finance then we cannot expect a minor here to go to work instead of school and ponder his/her personal finance. Also, many countries doesn’t let you work by the age of 65. So, putting aside the times we mentioned, **you have an average of ~17.155 days to make personal finance a part of your life.**

As I mentioned above, all these days has an economic impact on you. And you have to take serious these effects on your daily life.

Whether you are a wealthy, high-income person or living in poverty, you must keep track your personal finance to not to lose your way in these limited days. Then a question comes to mind:

> ***What metrics do I need to keep track?***

Tracking your financial situation periodically gives you a better point of view. Collecting your financial data at the end of each month and turning it into a table or graph will allow you to look at them and get inspiring results. If any, it will show your mistakes or things that went wrong. But first of all, you should set up your system in a few steps.

### 💼 What data do you have?

The most important factor in the records you will prepare is which data you will reveal to yourself. All the inferences you will make will be based on these data, and you will look at your own financial situation with the same data.

Although the number of these data is not large, I use the following metrics when creating financial tables myself each month:

- Monthly income
- Monthly expenses
- Total and type of investments (Silver, Fund, Stocks etc.)
- Currency (EUR, USD or JPY)
- Hours you work in a month (This is also a resource for anyone whose time is not infinite)

### 📊 How to use effectively the data we have?

With these numbers there’s plenty of inferences you can make. By doing that, you are able to see your investments is going up or down against a foreign currency or how much did your investments gain/lose in a month?

If you’re doing something wrong, it just appears in a few months by keeping track of this simple dataset.

It not only warns you of things going wrong, but also gives you ideas on how you can get better. There are chances of making the inference that the fund you received is below your expectations despite the passage of months. In such a case, you will change your investment areas and follow them for a few months and ultimately find the ideal investment type for you.

All this data can help you calculate other data. In my opinion, it is very useful to make a list of several items and follow them regularly:

- Net profit from investments
- Percentages of your income going towards expenses and investments
- The equivalent of your income in foreign currencies
- Percentage distribution of your investment portfolio and its monthly changes
- The amount of tax you pay (such as income tax)
