I’m a software engineer still in the early stages of my career. Throughout my work experience, I tried to do my best even if it was not needed that much.

I have been working on beautiful projects for nearly 3 years. And each of them I learned many things about various concepts in the endless software engineering world. The main idea of this blog: **These are not enough.**

The career path is like a race track where many people with different backgrounds race with each other. Some of them are not aware of that situation. Just moving without realizing that they are in this race with us. The race is not about just learning, obviously. Race is just about improving your skills and mentally or physically staying healthy while doing that.

Learning something new is an atomic way to navigate your career in this race. The important thing is that already everyone learns something new while working, especially in the early stages of their career. If you don’t learn a new thing or improve your knowledge in an area of work last week, it means you are being overtaken by others we can also call it the job market.

My conclusion from my own experience is that if you want to progress on this path faster than usual, you must continue to learn when others normally do not learn anything new, and I will call that **Continuous Learning**. What are these times when others don’t learn? (Not while you sleep). In ideal conditions, you should sleep 8 hours, work 8 hours, and do what you want to do for 8 hours in a single day. Sometimes you sleep less, and work more but at least 6 hours is yours in your day. I’m not going to say learn new things in these hours. It’s not that easy.

## What to learn?

Learning is not like just entering Wikipedia or your favorite news website and reading random things in random order. You should have a goal, and a path to follow.

For example, if you are a software engineer, or you want to become one, there are websites like [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/) where you can follow pre-defined paths and reach your goal without thinking of going the wrong way.

Divide your journey into many tiny parts and track your progress on the road. This will give you the ability to schedule your continuous learning progress. If you decide what to do, then continue to the next step.

## Build a schedule and stick to it

After finding what you are going to learn, and what way you choose, then you must build a schedule that is suitable for you.

Record when you go to sleep and when you wake up, and if you are not working remotely, record what time you leave work and arrive home. In light of this information, find the 1–2 hour period that is most comfortable for you. For me, this is usually 1.5 hours before bed. This is the time I find myself most productive outside of work.

After dealing with these time blocks, try yourself for a week. Grab your laptop or papers and take 1.5 hours to move yourself forward, even if only a little, from the previous day.

To give an example from myself, for the last 2 years of my career, I have spent every free time I felt productive in researching a technology I heard about in a project at work, examining the source codes of the libraries used in the project I was currently working on and contributing to the project, or developing a project in a new software language that I wanted to learn.

Building a schedule for yourself is hard, sticking to it is even harder. But after doing the dishes, you can sit comfortably, perhaps start a timer, and set aside 1.5 hours while drinking your favorite beverage. If you focus for 1.5 hours every day, then every week, and every weekday for a year, **you will have a total learning difference of 390 hours over your competitors** in this career race.

This difference, which cannot be ignored, will not make you a senior in 1 year. However, there will be a serious difference between you and people with the same experience. You can use this difference in the new job you are looking for or in your current job.
