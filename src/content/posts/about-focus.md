For several years, I have been engaging in remote work from the comfort of my home as a software engineer. Over time, I sought methods to sustain concentration during work sessions. In a digital landscape saturated with numerous distracting apps, notification sounds, and visual interruptions on our beloved mobile phones, maintaining focus on essential tasks has become a significant challenge.

In this blog, my intention is not merely to enumerate various techniques for maintaining focus. Instead, I aim to articulate my understanding of focus and elucidate the reasons behind the necessity to cultivate it while working, in order to achieve productivity and effectiveness in our daily, weekly, and monthly endeavors.

## What is Focus?

According to the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/focus), the focus is:

> Careful attention that is given to something such as a task, or the ability to give your full attention to something

The task represents the primary point to which you dedicate your attention at any given time of the day. This could be the work assigned by your manager or a challenging problem encountered in the course of your job. In my own terms, focus is a mental state where an individual directs their full attention toward a singular task.

Personally, I don’t consider myself a multi-tasker, so my definition of focus revolves around dedicating myself to a single task, often associated with my job or hobbies. Those who possess the ability to multi-task can effectively concentrate on more than one task simultaneously — a valuable skill in today’s environment where individuals are often expected to juggle multiple responsibilities.

### Is Focusing Necessary?

Certainly, multitasking is a common practice in our daily lives, and it’s generally acceptable for less demanding activities. However, when faced with a substantial task, especially one related to a job, splitting focus between two points is not conducive to optimal performance. I hold the belief that accomplishing a substantial amount of work, or even a significant portion of a complex task, requires sustained and undivided attention.

Engaging in activities such as checking social media accounts every couple of minutes while simultaneously composing an email to a colleague regarding meeting details is, in my view, impractical. The demand for constant attention to intricate and crucial tasks contradicts the effectiveness of attempting to divide focus across unrelated activities.

### Enemies of Focus

Focus can be likened to a substance with liquid properties, challenging to consolidate and easily susceptible to dissolution by external elements — these elements act as enemies or solvents to the state of focus. In my perspective, the two most significant adversaries to focus on are other people and social media.

When referring to other people, it specifically involves individuals you know, such as friends. Maintaining concentration becomes challenging when surrounded by a group of 30 or more friends, contrasting with the relative ease of focusing in a coffee shop filled with strangers. The potential for conversations, rather than just the presence of people, poses a distraction.

Social media stands out as another formidable disruptor of the focus state. While health and other factors contribute, they are not the primary focus of this blog. The act of staring at an OLED screen refreshing over 60 times per second and mindlessly scrolling through friends’ posts can be detrimental to achieving a focused and productive state. It hinders the ability to concentrate on meaningful tasks.

Here are some basic suggestions to counter these challenges:

1. **Implement Time Blocks:** Designate specific time blocks for your tasks or focused sessions. Further insights on this approach can be found in [this article.](/posts/balancing-your-life-between-contexts/)

2. **Minimize Mobile Device Distractions:** During these focused blocks, refrain from looking at your mobile devices unless the usage is directly related to your current task. This simple step can significantly enhance your ability to concentrate on the task at hand.

## How to Focus on something?

There are certain stages of focus, such as a starting point. Just do an activity to give it a start for your session. Start a timer, brew a cup of coffee, or use the [Forest App](https://www.forestapp.cc/). Sometimes I do all of that together, telling my brain “It’s time to start”, it has a big effect on me. Psychologically, it gives you a signal to do a common and frequent task automatically, like cycling. I use that technique all the time in my daily life.

It’s easier to start than continue to this concentration state. As a human, we always search for fun at any second. It makes a bit hard to stay focused on something and I don’t think that it has a solution for me. Playing some Lofi background music or listening to piano tracks on a loop helps sometimes.

## Conclusion

Focus is a delicate state that can easily be disrupted by external factors, acting as solvents to its effectiveness. The two major adversaries identified are other people, particularly friends, and the pervasive influence of social media. The potential for chatter and the constant stimulation of scrolling through posts on mobile devices can significantly hinder one’s ability to concentrate on meaningful tasks.

To address these challenges, implementing time blocks for tasks and focused sessions is recommended. Additionally, minimizing distractions from mobile devices during these blocks can greatly enhance concentration. By recognizing and actively managing these focus enemies, individuals can foster a more productive and effective work environment. Ultimately, the quest for sustained focus involves a conscious effort to create a conducive mental space amid the myriad distractions of the modern world.
