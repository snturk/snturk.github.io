Packages are maybe the most useful things in people’s life *to pack things*. When I say “packages”, I don’t just mean “packages”. For example, your bag is a very useful package too so it pack things. Even your notes is a package for your thoughts.

If we go for upper level, the packaging process doesn’t have to be three-dimensional either. You don’t have to cover each side, we can also consider the carpet, which can be assumed to be two-dimensional package. In your living room a carpet is a package that packs your table, seats or other furnitures on it. It covers them in a different way: Sets the borders of what it contains. Like carpets, sheets are packages for letters or even your hand is a package for what it holds inside.

All kinds of packages (both 2D and 3D) have one thing in common: Borders. So, what is the benefit of it?

***It holds together.*** For example, your table is a package for your laptop, monitor, notes and maybe a clock. It holds it together in another way. It describes the area of work or study. So I believe packing things up not only keeps them together physically, but also provides some comfort to your brain. However, on our computers we also enclose files in folders, separate them in relevant directories, that is, in packages.

If you are about to do a homework, it is good to keep all related files to your current activity in a folder. It describes the strict bounds of your *workspace*. By doing that, your brain easily focus on what you are doing on that time. At that time, you don’t need what’s *outside*.

***It provides singularity.***In my life, I care much about singularity, both my career and personal life, singularity is sometimes a lifesaver style. At a time period, I always try to do a *single task*. I am aware of multi-tasking is another way of working but I believe it is not effective as “single-tasking”. Packaging serves this purpose in a good way. In single tasking, you have to focus on single thing, for me (as a software developer) it is just a package or workspace that I like to call. This package can be a Safari Tab, a block of code, a paper or a meeting with a colleague.

***It creates you time.*** You can package your time too. Even if you are a busy person, you can still create additional time by managing your day better. I do it with a method: Time Packaging. Packing time means keeping the time you need for activities clearly separate without doing more than one activity at the same time. I find it very useful to visualize your time while organizing it. Using any calendar application on your computer or even just using pen and paper, listing what you will do that day and then dividing them into *blocks* is a very common method that will allow you to strengthen the *single-task principle* I mentioned above during the day. It also brings a flow to your day if you divide all of your time as blocks that glued to each other.

Unfortunately, time management is a deep subject and contains details that cannot fit into a single paragraph. So it’s maybe another and this can be covered on its own in another blog post.

### 🧠 Final Thought

Packaging is a life organizing tool that wants you to divide and re-organize your tasks. I enjoy using it at work and in my daily life for a couple years and find it productive. In this first blog, I tried to convey this packaging concept to you. I hope you liked it 🌟
